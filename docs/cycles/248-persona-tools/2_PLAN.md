# Plan: the persona's tools, its own origin, the audit that says who, and the stop

## What this plan is

The approved specification (`1_SPEC.md`, gate 1 passed on 2026-10-11: every open question settled with its recommendation; the excluded channels, the test-workspace switch and the test-secret confirmations among them, stay out of the persona's reach; the stop is the person's; the schema stays 30; a fixed, app-worded footer goes on the text the persona sends to the code host) asks for four things: **tools** over what a person can do in the app, a **third call origin** so nothing the persona does reads as the person's, an **audit** that says who acted and what it had read, and **one stop**. This document says where each piece lands in this tree (release `0.10.0-beta.1`, schema 30), in which order it is built, and which tests hold it. Every `file:line` below was read again on this tree. It builds on #247 (the persona, its chat, `src/main/persona/`) and #246 (turn limits that ask, `src/main/turnAsks*.ts`).

Seven things the reading changed or settled, none of them a product decision:

1. **The test-workspace refusal is recorded by the door, not by the handler.** Spec rule 35 and acceptance 16 say the handler records the refusal with `ok: false`. `approveAction` and `audited()` call `assertExternalWrite` before anything is written (`src/main/actions.ts:495-496,538-539`), and so do the board's writes (`src/main/board.ts:278-290,326-350`), so a refused act leaves **no** handler line. The door's `act` line (rule 18) is what records it, with `ok: false` and the usual message. Nothing the spec asks for changes; the line comes from another place.
2. **The board's file is not open in a test workspace.** Rule 35 lists "the board's file" among the local things the persona can still do there. Every board write calls `assertExternalWrite` first (same lines), for the person too. The door adds no guard and removes none, so the persona gets exactly the person's result; acceptance 16 already says "a board write ... refused".
3. **`Settings` writes through `config:save`, not `config:cycle-save`.** `config:cycle-save` refuses what a phone may not change (`src/main/configModule.ts:68-74`, `refusedPaths` at `configScope.ts:136-138`); that is the device's limit, which rule 2 says the persona does not have. The op builds the whole next configuration from one path (rule 10) and sends it to `config:save` (`configModule.ts:61-64`), which validates it like any save. `config:cycle-save` goes on the exclusion list with that reason.
4. **`id` of rule 17 is the act's id**, shared by every line the act writes (an approval's several host writes, its `config` line). The chat links to the act; the Audit screen scrolls to its first line.
5. **The door-only list has four entries, not two.** Rule 4 allows a third kind (a read that aggregates what waits for the person) and asks the plan to list it: `Asks.list`, and `Secrets.list` (the secrets store's names and sources; the only channel that carries them, `config:get`, belongs to `Settings.get`, and a channel is named by one op). The four are `Secrets.read`, `Secrets.list`, `Board.proposePriority`, `Asks.list`.
6. **The count of channels.** 298 are registered today: 274 literal names plus 24 through constants (`VOICE_CHANNELS` 7 in `src/shared/voiceSetup.ts:7-15`, `WIZARD_CHANNELS` 15 in `src/shared/wizard.ts:24-40`, `ACTIVITY_GET` in `src/shared/activity.ts:52`, `PERSONA_LIVE_READ`). This issue adds three (`persona:stop`, `persona:resume`, `persona:state`): **301** classified.
7. **The origin of a message is read where the post is made.** The spec could not say whether the door a message came by survives the chain of promises that answers it. By reading: the forum store emits to its subscribers synchronously inside `append` (`src/main/forum-core.ts:301-309,412`), so the listener at `src/main/mentions/module.ts:102` runs inside the post's own call and can read the origin there; the plan reads it there and carries it in the answer's dependencies, and relies on the async store for nothing after that. Not executed.

The shape of the answer, in eleven sentences:

- **One door.** `personaDoor` (`src/main/persona/door.ts`) runs **the handler of a channel** (the function the window's own call runs) under the persona origin, so validation, the test-workspace guard, the handler's own audit line, the error log and the activity job stay where they are. It adds the stop, the record of what was read, the act line in the chat and the audit stamp.
- **A pinned table** (`src/main/persona/doorTable.ts`) puts each of the 301 channels either in exactly one tool operation or on an exclusion list with a reason; a test reads the sources and fails for a channel that is in neither, in both, or in a list but registered nowhere.
- **Eleven tools**, engine-neutral `ToolImpl`s given to the persona's real entry in `runnerTools`, flat schemas with an `op`, per-operation validation in the main process, a required `why` on every write, `principalOnly`, and a guard hook on the Claude path.
- **A third origin.** `callOrigin()` returns `ipc`, `web` or `persona`; one helper, `viaOf()`, replaces the six `=== 'web'` comparisons and a scan test forbids a seventh.
- **An act scope** (an `AsyncLocalStorage` in a leaf module) is open while a door call runs and closed when its handler settles; `recordWrite` stamps every line written while it is open with `actor`, `door`, `id`, `said`, `basis`.
- **Four new audit kinds** (`config`, `secret`, `plugin-decision`, `act`) are written for every origin, with paths and safe values only.
- **What it read** is two parts: a `subject` the door reads from the app's own state at the moment of the act, and up to eight of the persona's own tool results, masked and bounded.
- **The chat shows each act** as a durable system line worded by the app at display time, capped at thirty per answer, linked to the audit.
- **The stop** is a latch file outside the configuration, read at start and failing closed; `persona:stop|resume|state` open to the phone, not in the door's table; the stop aborts every call registered in the persona's thread, and the door refuses an in-flight tool call in the same tick it would start the handler.
- **Records worded as the person's** take a `who` parameter that defaults to the person, and the persona's door sets it.
- **The text the persona sends to the code host** gets a fixed footer, added by the door to the fields the table marks as host text.

## The door

### The origin, and the helper that names it

```ts
// src/shared/auditoria.ts
export type Through = 'window' | 'paired' | 'persona';

// src/main/rpc.ts
const origin = new AsyncLocalStorage<'ipc' | 'web' | 'persona'>();
/** 'persona' only while the persona's act is open: work its handler leaves running is not the persona's (rule 13). */
export const callOrigin = (): 'ipc' | 'web' | 'persona' => { const o = origin.getStore() ?? 'ipc'; return o === 'persona' && !currentAct()?.open ? 'ipc' : o; };
export const viaOf = (): Through => { const o = callOrigin(); return o === 'web' ? 'paired' : o === 'persona' ? 'persona' : 'window'; };
export function invokePersona(channel: string, args: unknown[]): unknown;  // guarded(channel, 'persona', fn, args), the same wrapper
```

- `guarded` (`rpc.ts:23`) takes `via: 'ipc' | 'web' | 'persona'`; the error log's `rpcContext(channel, args, via)` (`errorlog-core.ts:213`) widens to the same union, so a failure of a persona act is logged with `via: persona`. The HTTP door still labels its calls `web` (`rpc.ts:66-75`), and `bindIpc` still labels `ipc` (`:53-56`).
- `invokePersona` looks the channel up in the same `table` (`rpc.ts:12`); a channel that is only in `deviceTable` (the `push:` ones) is not reachable (they are on the exclusion list). It refuses any channel that starts with `persona:` before it looks, as a second line behind the table (the table is the first).
- **The six comparisons become `viaOf()`**: `turnAsks.ts:56` (`TurnAnswerer`), `runner/module.ts:128` (`auditMove`) and `:383` (`AnsweredThrough`), `memory/module.ts:35` (the memory audit's `via`), `persona/assist.ts:38` (`through`) and `:261` (the scope check, now `viaOf() === 'paired'`, so the persona is never held to a device's scope). `AnsweredThrough` (`browser/audit.ts:18`) and `TurnAnswerer` (`shared/turnLimit.ts:14`, whose comment already leaves room) gain `persona`; `MemoryAuditInput.via` (`memory/audit.ts:14`) and the editor's `through` take `Through`.
- **A test fails when any file under `src/main` other than `rpc.ts` mentions `callOrigin`** (`test/persona-origin.test.ts`). `test/runs-policy.test.ts:56` pins the old text of `auditMove`; it is changed to pin `viaOf()` there.

### The act scope

`src/main/persona/act.ts` is a leaf module (only `node:async_hooks`) so `rpc.ts` and `auditoria.ts` can import it without a cycle:

```ts
export interface Act {
  open: boolean;
  id: string;                 // `a-<time36>-<n>`, one per door call that writes
  door: string;               // 'window' | 'paired': how the message the answer began from came in; open to more values (#250's schedule)
  said: string;               // the model's `why`, masked, at most 300
  basis: ActBasis;            // rule 22, built by the door before the handler starts
  mask: (text: string) => string;   // the answer's exact-value mask (rule 9)
  wrote: { count: number; kinds: Set<AuditKind> };   // what the handler wrote: decides whether the door writes the `act` and `config` lines
}
export const runAct: <T>(act: Act, fn: () => T) => T;
export const currentAct: () => Act | null;
```

`recordWrite` (`auditoria.ts:24`) reads `currentAct()`; when it is open it merges `actor: 'persona'`, `door`, `id`, `said`, `basis` **after** the entry's own fields (a handler cannot override them), counts the line in `wrote`, and bounds and masks `basis` (below). The door sets `open = false` in a `finally` once the handler's promise has settled (rule 13). A run the persona starts keeps writing its own lines after that, unstamped (acceptance 6). Lines it writes **before** the handler settles, as part of the act (the first steps of a run started inside `runs:start`), are the act's, and are stamped; the test pins both sides.

### The run of one call

`door.run(session, tool, input)` does, in this order:

1. **Find the operation.** An unknown `op` is a `ToolError` that lists the operations and says "stopping and resuming the persona are the person's; nothing here changes them" (the answer to a persona that asks to resume, acceptance 3).
2. **Validate the arguments** against `args` (types, required, lengths, ids by their regexes, `json` values at most 20,000 characters when serialised), and `why` (1 to 300 characters) on a write. Nothing is attempted on a failure; no audit line (rule 20), a tool error the model reads.
3. **Prepare, with awaits allowed:** `build` the channel's arguments (resolve a card, read the configuration and apply the path, add the footer to `hostText` fields), compute the `subject` from the app's own state, and make the `basis` (below).
4. **The latch, then the handler, in one synchronous stretch.** `if (latch.stopped()) refuse; registerInflight(act); const p = runAct(act, () => invokePersona(channel, args));` with no `await` between the check and the handler's first line (rule 30.3). The refusal is a tool error ("the persona is stopped") and no handler runs.
5. **Settle.** On success or failure: if the handler wrote no line (`act.wrote.count === 0`) and the op is a write, the door writes the `act` line (target `Tool.op`, `ok`, a masked reason on failure, the `subject` as the origin summary); if the configuration changed (a reference compare of `getConfig()` before and after, then `changedPaths`) and the handler wrote no `config` line, it writes a `config` line; then the act line in the chat; then `act.open = false`; then it unregisters the in-flight act.
6. **Return text for the model:** the result masked with `redact` and the answer's value mask, cut with `clip` (`src/main/engine/open/tools/types.ts:77`) at 12,000 characters, and, for an `outside` op, inside the `<data>` fence the prompts use (`fence` escapes a `<data` inside the text, `src/main/runner/prompt.ts:121`; the wrapping is the template's, as in `call.ts:221`). A read adds a record to the answer's ring of reads; a write does not.

An act needs nothing from the phone's policy: `webAccess` is not consulted anywhere in the door (rule 2), and a test pins that `door.ts` does not import `webPolicy`.

**The session of an answer** (`AnswerSession`, made in `answerMentions` for the real persona): the persona's id, the thread, the message's `seq`, the `door` the message came by, the answer's `MaskSet` (the existing `createMaskSet`, `src/main/browser/mask.ts:16`), the ring of reads (the last forty, in memory), and the count of act lines written. The mask is made where the call's line is made (`mentions/module.ts:118`) so the activity (`beginCallActivity`, `src/main/activity.ts:369`, which gains an optional `mask` applied in `safeText`) and the answer share it.

## The eleven tools

- **Schema.** Each tool is `{ op: enum, why?: string, ...named arguments }`, flat, `additionalProperties: false`, one property per argument name across its operations (each with a description that says which operations use it). The description lists every operation in one line (what it does, and `[writes]` on those that do). Tool and argument texts are English prompt text, marked `i18n-ignore` the way `OpenScreen` marks its own (`src/main/persona/openScreen.ts:73-84`). The size of the eleven schemas in tokens is measured at commit 6 and written in the implementation notes (not measured now).
- **Validation is the door's** (step 2), because the Claude path turns every declared property into `z.unknown()` (`src/main/runner/tools.ts:174-176`) and the model's `enum` and `required` are not enforced there. `runnerMcpServer` is changed to pass each property's description (`.describe`) the way the memory's tools do (`src/main/memory/engineTool.ts:46-47`), or the Claude model would never read the argument texts.
- **Offered to the real persona's entry only.** In `answer.ts:435`, where `OpenScreen` is added, the eleven are added when `persona && !isDraft(def) && deps.persona && !deps.chain?.length`. Never to the test copy (`isDraft`), never to another agent whatever its permission, never to an agent the persona called (`deps.chain`), and not to a ceremony place. `allowedTools` follows `runnerTools` on the open engine (`agents.ts:676`) and on the Claude one (`:816`).
- **Both engines, same names.** The open engine runs the `ToolImpl`s directly; the Claude SDK gets them in the in-process server named `runner` (`runner/tools.ts:197`), as `mcp__runner__<Name>` (`:195`).
- **The sub-agent guard.** Each tool is `principalOnly: true`. The open engine honours it (`src/main/engine/open/loop.ts:426,477`: the sub-agent's `allowedTools` drops the name). The SDK path has no such flag, so a `PreToolUse` hook is added next to the memory's (`agents.ts:860`): `{ matcher: <the mcp names of every runner tool marked principalOnly>, hooks: [principalOnlyGuard] }`, which denies when the hook input carries `agent_id`. It sits in `runner/tools.ts` beside `runnerMcpToolName`, and it also makes `OpenScreen` (already `principalOnly`, `openScreen.ts:90`) hold on the Claude path. Whether the SDK fires the hook for a sub-agent's call to an in-process tool is unverified, here as for the memory (`engineTool.ts:57-60`): a manual check before commit 6 is merged settles it (below, "The real checks").
- **Results.** Text for the model, never JSON of the whole object: each op has a `render` that keeps the fields a person would read (ids, titles, states, the first lines of a text) and drops the rest, then the cut. Text from outside the app (an issue's body, a comment, a thread, a page's step, a plugin's note, a memory note an agent wrote) is returned in the fence; the table marks those ops `outside`.
- **A refusal** (a handler's, the test workspace's, the door's) is a `ToolError` with the app's own words; it is what the model reads and what the act line shows.

### `Settings.set`, one path at a time (rule 10)

`{ path, value, mode?: 'set' | 'append' | 'remove', why }`. The path grammar (`configPath.ts`, pure): dot-separated keys; a segment may be `name[id]` to pick the element with that `id` of an array of objects (`agents.team[persona].shell`, `squads[ops].members`); `__proto__`, `constructor` and `prototype` are refused; `set` replaces the value at the path (creating a key of an existing object, never an intermediate object), `append` pushes onto an array, `remove` deletes a key or an element. `build` reads `getConfig()`, clones it, applies the change, and hands the **whole** document to `config:save`, whose handler is the one that validates (`saveConfig` → `checkConfig`, `workspaceConfig.ts:88-110`) and applies it: the persona-entry rules of #247 (one persona, no stage, no squad, no `turnsTo`, no `autonomous`, no `system`) are validation for everyone and refuse a bad save with the usual message. Any other field may be changed, `agents.team[<persona>].permission|tracker|shell|screen|allowedHosts|browserProfile` and the autonomy blocks included. A long document never goes through the model. `Settings.get {path}` returns the slice at a path of `view().config` (`configModule.ts:21-31`) and never `view().secrets`. `settings:save` (the app settings: sound, theme, schedule) has the same shape, `Settings.setApp`, reading `getSettings()` and applying a path before `settings:save` (`src/main/index.ts:187-191`); its read-only fields are ignored by the save, as for the screen.

### What it read (rule 22)

- **`subject`** is read by the door at the moment of the act from the app's state, by op, in `subject.ts`: for `actions:approve` the action's `summary` and its command's method and endpoint (`describe`, `actions.ts:159`); for `runs:screenAnswer` the step in words and the site of the ask; for `plugins:answer` the plugin and the need; for a `Settings` write the paths about to change (no values); for `runs:gate`, `accept`, `return`, `answer` the run's issue and stage; for a post the thread's title; for the rest the tool, the op and the ids it was given. - **`reads`**: from the ring, up to eight, those whose target shares an id, ref or run with the subject first, then the newest; each `{ tool, op, target, excerpt }` with the excerpt at most 240 characters of the already-masked result; plus the `seq` of the message the answer began from. A secret's value, a typed hand-off value and the persona's instructions are never in a result the ring stores: the value read by `Secrets.read` is stored in the ring as `[secret <ref>]`, and `Settings.get` of the persona's `instructions` path is stored as its length.
- It is the persona's **tool results**, not its reasoning (the reasoning is a switch of the activity and memory-only).

### Values the persona read (rule 9)

`Secrets.read {ref}` calls `secrets().resolve(ref)` (`src/main/secrets-core.ts:142`), returns the value to the model and **adds the value to the answer's `MaskSet`**. The mask is then applied (by exact value, any length) to: the live text (`personaLive` is given `(text) => mask(redact(text))` for this answer through `live.begin`, `answer.ts:438`), the text posted (`answer.ts:447-451`, before `forum.append`), the stopped answer's partial (`:468`), the activity lines (the optional `mask`), the act line, the audit (`recordWrite`), the ring of reads and the error log's context. The `secret` line for a read records the ref and nothing else. A value shorter than the redaction can tell from ordinary text still garbles any text that contains it; that is accepted and listed in the risks.

## The stop

### State: the latch

`src/main/persona/latch.ts` (pure but for the file): `readLatch(dir)` returns `{ stopped, since, door }`. The file is `persona-latch.json` in the workspace's folder (`ATAS`, `src/main/env.ts:15`), **outside the configuration**, so `Settings` cannot reach it, an older app opens the workspace as before, and the schema does not change. **Absent file: not stopped. Unreadable, unparsable or of a shape it does not know: stopped** (fail closed). `stop(door)` sets the in-memory flag first and then writes the file (temporary file and rename, synchronous); `resume(door)` writes `{ stopped: false, ... }`. The in-memory flag is the truth while the app runs; the file is read once at start. It is published as the module event `persona:latch` (to the window and every paired browser, the way `PERSONA_LIVE_EVENT` is, `persona/module.ts:11`) and read with `persona:state`, which returns `{ stopped, since, door, waiting }` (`waiting`: the count of held messages).

### The channels, and who can use them

`persona:stop`, `persona:resume` and `persona:state` are registered by `personaModule` (`persona/module.ts`), added to `PERSONA_CHANNELS` (`webPolicy.ts:108`, the pattern at `:109` is built from the same set), open to a paired browser and **not** in `EXTERNAL_EFFECT` (`:24`): stopping takes capability away, and resuming is the person's act on their own persona. They are not in the door's table (the `own` exclusion) and `invokePersona` refuses a `persona:` channel. They are **not queueable** (`src/shared/outbox.ts:5`: a phone offline must not hold a Stop to replay later); a test pins it. Each writes an audit line with the existing `persona` kind (`persona/assist.ts:41-`, `target: 'persona:stop'` or `'persona:resume'`, the door from `viaOf()`), so the stop and the resume are findable.

### What "at once" does

`stop(door)` is one synchronous stretch before the channel returns:

1. sets the latch (memory, then file), so no new tool call starts (door step 4) and no new answer begins (the mentions module, below);
2. aborts every call registered in the persona's thread: `for (const c of callStops.running(thread)) callStops.stop(c.thread, c.agent)` (`src/main/mentions/stop.ts:29-35`). The persona's answer and every agent it called register under the same thread (`answer.ts:259`; `CallAgent` answers in the same thread), so one loop ends the model's request, its sub-agents (the abort reaches the engine, `answer.ts:253-258`), a question waiting at a turn limit (the asker holds `abort.signal`, `answer.ts:388`) and the shell session (`finally`, `answer.ts:483-500`). An answer the abort ended posts what it had written, as the per-answer Stop does (`answer.ts:468-476`); the per-answer `stopped` line (`:478`) is **not** written when the latch is the cause, because the next step writes the one line that says it;
3. reads `door.inflight()` (the door tracks each act between its step 4 and its settle) for the acts in flight;
4. posts `persona.stopped { door, count, inflight }` in the thread (`inflight`: `Tool.op subject` joined, masked, cut to 300), with the words "they finish on their own" in its text; writes the audit line; emits the event.

**Asks that wait for the person are not touched** (rule 31): a browser hold or confirmation, a plugin's request, a ceremony's command and a hand-off request stay pending for the person with their own clocks (a hold counts as declined after fifteen minutes); stopping answers nothing on their behalf. Only a question the persona's own answer was waiting on (a turn-limit decision) ends, because its answer ends.

What is already happening is **not undone** (rule 30): an approval or a push that had begun finishes, is audited, and the line named it. A tool call that is already on its way (the model sent it, the door is between step 2 and step 4) is refused at step 4. A tool call whose handler is awaiting its host is in flight and finishes; the door then writes its lines (they follow the stopped line in the thread) and returns the result to a model that is already aborted.

### Waiting messages, resume, restart

- **The mentions module** (`mentions/module.ts:102-146`) reads `viaOf()` first (item 7 above) and, for a message in the persona's thread, checks the latch. Stopped: the call line is made as `queued` (the existing state), and the message is pushed on an ordered in-memory list `held` with its place, calls and door; no chain is started. A message already chained behind the stopped answer is handled at the start of its step (`.then`, `:126`): it checks the latch again and, if stopped, joins `held` instead of calling `answerMentions`. The chat's `queuedOf` (`chatModel.ts`) shows a queued message as **waiting for Resume** while the state is stopped, **waiting its turn** otherwise.
- **Resume** clears the latch, posts `persona.resumed`, writes the audit line, emits the event, then re-schedules `held` **in order** through the same chaining function (so per-thread order is the existing `inFlight` one). It then counts the person's posts in the thread after the last `persona.stopped` line that no answer replied to (`answersTo`) and that are not in `held` (the app was restarted while stopped; the list is in memory) and, if there are any, posts one `persona.notAnswered { count }` line, for the person to write them again (rule 32).
- **Restart.** The latch is on disk, so a stopped persona stays stopped; `held` is gone; nothing runs from a request made before the stop (rule 38).
- **Clear and Delete** are refused while an answer is going or waiting (`answerPending`, `persona/assist.ts:273`) and are allowed while stopped with nothing going; a message in `held` counts as waiting, so it is added to that check and dropped when the thread is cleared.
- **A stopped persona is not a disabled one** (rule 34): the conversation, the editor, Clear and Delete, and every read of the app work; only the persona's answering and its tools are held.

### Where the control is

`StopControl.tsx` (one button: **Stop**, or **Resume** while stopped) in `ChatHeader.tsx` on the computer and the phone; a banner over the box in `ChatScreen.tsx` while stopped (the text of rule 33); `PersonaIndicator.tsx` in `App.tsx` beside the jobs dock for every other screen **while the persona works (`useAnyLive()`, `liveApi.ts:56`) or is stopped**, with the same control; `PersonaSection.tsx` shows the state and Resume. The DOM-free decisions are in `personaStopModel.ts` (`indicatorView`, `bannerView`, `controlOf`). It uses theme tokens only and `ui-persona` keys in both catalogs. The wizard's test copy is never stopped: it cannot act and the latch is only read for the real persona.

## Risks, and what was assumed and not verified

Nothing was executed in this stage: no test was run and no behaviour was seen. The lines were read; the design is a reading of them. The spec's own "not verified" list is carried here with what the reading settled.

1. **The origin of a queued message** (spec). Read, not run: the forum emits synchronously inside `append` (`forum-core.ts:412`), so the mentions listener sees the post's own origin. If a path ever appended from a detached task, the door would read `ipc`; the test posts through `invoke` (`web`) and through the IPC wrapper and pins both.
2. **Async work and the act's scope.** The scope closes when the handler's promise settles. Work a handler leaves running keeps the async store, which is why `callOrigin()` and `recordWrite` consult `act.open` and not the store alone. A handler that returns before a lazily-started step writes (a timer set inside it) is outside the act; the test uses a run that writes after the handler returned.
3. **The run's history takes `persona` as `by`.** `HistoryEntry.by` is typed `string` (`shared/runs/types.ts:146`; written by `log`, `transitions.ts:36`) and `RUN_VERSION` is untouched, so no migration; no code was found that validates `by` against a list, but `suggestions.ts`, `activities.ts` and `prompt.ts` read it and are in the table.
4. **Code that treats a run-thread message by its author type** (spec). Two found (`runner/memory.ts:92`, `forum-core.ts:363`); the search at commit 5 reads every site again. A persona-authored answer that a place ignores because its author is an agent is the failure to look for.
5. **The Claude path and the guard** (spec). Whether the SDK reports a sub-agent's call to an in-process tool to a `PreToolUse` hook with `agent_id` is unverified, as for the memory (`engineTool.ts:57-60`). The real check settles it; the fallback is the open engine alone.
6. **The Claude path has no abort signal in a tool** (`runner/tools.ts:200` builds the context with none). The stop does not depend on one: the door carries the latch, and the abort of the query ends the model's side. A handler already running is in flight by design.
7. **Redaction is not complete** (spec). A secret that is not shaped like a token is caught only by the exact-value mask, and only after the persona read it through `Secrets.read`. A secret the persona was *told* some other way (typed by the person in the chat) is masked by the app's `redact` and nothing else. A secret shorter than a few characters garbles any text that holds the same characters when masked by value; the read is not refused for it.
8. **A `command` secret is a command the app runs.** `Secrets.set` with source `command` stores a command the app runs on this computer when the secret is used; the `secret` line records the ref and the type, not the command. It adds to the host reach the persona has by changing `runner.commands` or giving itself `shell: host`; both are "no fixed limits", and the `raised` mark covers the second.
9. **`raised` covers four checks, not every grant.** `raisedPermissions`, `poolRaised` and `raisedAutonomy` do not look at `runner.autonomy` (the workspace block), `allowedCommands` or `runner.commands`; those paths are in the line's `paths` but not marked `raised`. Question 2.
10. **`ata:save` and `ata:teams` take the renderer's `Minutes` object** (`src/main/index.ts:176-177`). The operation takes the object as JSON and the door validates its shape from the type; whether the handler itself validates a model-written one was not read. If it does not, the operation is limited to the day's saved minutes and the decision is recorded in the implementation notes.
11. **The persona's tool use on weaker models** is not measured: a flat schema with an `op` enum and eleven tools is the design for a short list, but how the models of the open engine use it is seen only in the first real session. The size of the schemas in tokens is measured at commit 6.
12. **A host shell the persona gave itself ends the guarantees** (spec). It can edit `persona-latch.json`, the audit file and the secrets file. The latch is read once at start and held in memory, so deleting the file while the app runs does not resume; after a restart an absent file is "not stopped". The stop and the audit are limits of the app's doors.
13. **A write already sent cannot be taken back** (spec). The stopped line names the acts in flight; the rest is in the audit.
14. **An older build's Audit screen** looks a line's kind up in a table and does not draw one it does not know. A workspace written by this version opens in the previous build (same schema) and its audit would not draw the new kinds there. Not changed in the previous build; the new screen falls back to the raw kind.
15. **An audit that grows** (spec). Every configuration change and every persona act adds a line and the file is never swept; no sweep is added. `basis` is bounded per line at 2,000 characters, so the growth is bounded per act.
16. **The queue is in memory** (spec). A restart while stopped loses the held list; the count line at Resume says how many messages were not answered.
17. **The renderer is not rendered by any test.** `StopControl`, `PersonaIndicator`, the act line and the Audit screen's changes are compiled (`npx electron-vite build`) and tested through their DOM-free models; the layout at 360 px and the indicator over the jobs dock are not seen until the run of the app.
18. **A card created on the board keeps the footer** in its stored body when it is never sent (above).
19. **Guard scans read source by regular expression** (the registration scan, the `callOrigin` scan, the worded-sites scan). A refactor that changes how a registration or an author is written can slip past until the next review; they are as strong as the memory's and the team readers'.
20. **The persona's number of tools in context.** Eleven tools with about 190 operations in their descriptions are in the prompt of every persona answer. The cost per answer is not measured.
