# Persona tools: acts over everything a person can do, an origin of its own, an audit that says who, and one stop

## What is asked

Issue #248, the second of the five that make up #245 (a persona of the person that acts in the app on their behalf). #247 gave the persona its kind, its wizard and the chat that is the home; today it chats, reads like a reading agent, and offers buttons (`OpenScreen`). This one gives it hands, and the means to see and stop them:

- **Tools.** Engine-neutral tools, offered to both engines as `coxia_memory` and `CallAgent` are, over the app's own actions: runs, gates and answers; the board and priorities; conversations and memory; settings and configuration (the team, autonomy and its own permissions included); secrets; approvals in Actions; plugin decisions; the browser's holds; ceremonies. Each tool has a schema and a description.
- **A third call origin**, `persona`, so nothing it does reads as the person's.
- **An audit that says who**, with what it read when it decided.
- **One stop**, on the computer and on the phone: it halts what the persona is doing at once and keeps it from acting until the person resumes it.

## What the maintainer settled

Product decisions from the conversation that opened the epic, restated in #247 and #248. They are recorded here as settled; this spec does not reopen them.

1. **No fixed limits.** The persona can do everything a person can do in the app, including approving its own proposals, changing its own permissions, reading and setting secrets, answering the browser's holds of irreversible steps and the plugins' permission requests.
2. **It works from the paired phone too**, with the same reach.
3. **What is kept is visibility and control:** an origin of its own, an audit that says who and what it read, and one stop.
4. **Gates keep their autonomy**; nothing here changes how a gate is decided. Voice (#249), schedules (#250) and plugins, MCP and secrets by reference (#251) are the next issues; the budget of #198 is separate.

## What changes for the person

- Ask the persona in the chat to do something the app can do ("approve the push of the release", "put #123 first", "answer the browser's question with no", "turn the sound off") and it does it, through the same actions the screens use. Under its answer, **a line per act** says what it did, whether it worked, and links to the audit line.
- The **Audit** screen tells a person's acts from the persona's: a badge, a filter, the door the request came by (the window or the phone), the persona's one-sentence reason, and, folded, **what it had read** when it decided.
- A **Stop** button is on the chat's header on the computer and on the phone, and on a small indicator on every screen while the persona is working. Pressing it halts the persona's answer, its calls to other agents and its next tool call, and keeps it stopped: messages written meanwhile wait, with a banner, until **Resume** (the same button).
- Everything else about the app is as before. A person who never talks to the persona sees only the Audit's new filter and the config, secret and plugin lines, which are now written for the person's own changes too.

## Rules

### The tools

1. **One door, in the main process.** The persona acts through a single door, `personaDoor`. A tool's operation names the channel it wraps, and the door runs **that channel's handler**, the function the window's own call runs, under the persona origin (rule 12). It does not go through the phone's HTTP path, and it does not call the services directly. The reason is that validation, the test-workspace guard, the handler's own audit, the error log and the activity job live in the handler, once; a tool that called a service would copy them and miss the next one. What the door adds, no handler has: it refuses when the persona is stopped (rule 28), it keeps the record of what was read (rule 22), it words the act's line in the chat (rule 24) and it stamps the audit (rule 18).
2. **The phone's policy is not applied to the persona.** `webAccess` is not consulted for the persona's calls: it is the decision that the persona has no fixed limits, and the phone's limits (a desktop-only channel, the external-effects switch, `WEB_EDITABLE`) are the ones the maintainer set for a *device*. The only limits of the door are the exclusions of rule 3 and the stop.
3. **The table is exhaustive and pinned.** Every registered channel is either named by exactly one tool operation or is on an **exclusion list with its reason**; a channel added with neither fails a test, as an unlisted `persona:` channel is denied to the phone today. The exclusions are what makes no sense for an agent or would make it the machine's owner rather than the person's representative (Q2): the window and the machine (`autostart:`, `clipboard:`, `claude:`, `jobs:notify`, `log:renderer`, `state:`), the update and its install (`update:`), voice and its installs (`voice:`), the setup wizard and native dialogs (`wizard:`, `config:export`, `config:import-*`, `mcpstate:`, `vcs:probe`, `sandbox:probe`), the person's hands on an agent's screen (`screen:`), the pairing of devices (`web:`, `push:`), the switch of workspace and its deletion, the persona's own `clear` and `delete` (already refused while an answer is going, which is every moment it can call them), and the new stop channels (rule 29). The test workspace's own switch and the test-environment confirmations are listed here **as a proposal that departs from the letter of "no limits"** (Q2).
4. **Door-only operations.** Two operations have no channel and are named in the table with the rest: reading a secret's value (rule 9) and proposing a priority change on a tracker activity (a label change, the proposal `ata:save` makes today for a saved decision, `src/main/store.ts:123-141`; for a card of the board, `board:update` already carries the priority). A third kind of thing the door-only list may hold is a read that aggregates what waits for the person; the plan lists any it needs, and a test pins the list.
5. **Eleven tools, by area, one `op` each** (Q1). Names follow `OpenScreen` and `CallAgent`.

    | Tool | Reads | Acts (channels it wraps) |
    |---|---|---|
    | `Overview` | cards, the cycle, history, cost, the day, health, errors, radar, watchers, the audit (read-only, its own lines included) | dismiss a watcher alert, run the radar |
    | `Runs` | list, get, activities, artifacts, evidence, screens | `runs:start`, `startStage`, `startRelease`, `accept`, `return`, `gate`, `answer`, `command`, `retry`, `retryPr`, `cancel`, `skipWait`, `sendBack`, `migrateFlow`, `undoPost`, `setSquad`, `removeSquad`, `setSquadAutonomous`, `setAutonomous`, `activitySave`, `memory`, `docs:start` |
    | `Actions` | list, preview | `actions:approve`, `skip`, `detect`, `freeBranch`, the `conflict:` flow |
    | `Board` | list | `board:create`, `send`, `sendAll`, `update` (priority included), `comment`, `close`, `reopen`; the door-only priority proposal |
    | `Conversations` | list, read, attachments | `forum:post`, `create`, `attachment-delete`; posts are the persona's (rule 15) |
    | `Memory` | the memory, the learned procedures, their figures | `memory:save`, `review`, `remove`, `remove-folder`; `procedures:save`, `review`, `restore`, `delete`, `offer-keep`, `offer-decline` |
    | `Settings` | `config:get`, `schema`, `validate`, `prompts`, `settings:get`, templates | `config:save`, `config:cycle-save` (one path of the configuration at a time, rule 10), `prompt-set`, `settings:save`, `cycle:apply` and the template channels, `agents:apply`, the workspace's list and creation |
    | `Secrets` | the list, the check | `config:secret-set`, `secret-remove`, `secrets-accept-insecure`; the door-only read of a value |
    | `Plugins` | `plugins:list` | `plugins:answer`, `revoke`, `revoke-write`, `set-enabled`, `settings`, `set-setting`, `set-secret` |
    | `Asks` | everything that waits for the person: browser holds and confirmations, hand-off requests, turn-limit questions, ceremony commands, run questions | `runs:screenAnswer`, `handoffDecline`, `screenClose`, `turnAsks:answer`, `ceremony:command` |
    | `Ceremonies` | `gate:get`, `qa:get`, `retro:latest`, the minutes, re-entry and discussions | the ceremonies' main-side channels: `gate:`, `qa:`, `retro:`, `deep:`, `feedback:`, `ata:save`, `minutes:` writes |

    The pre-daily itself is **not** a tool: it is held on the screen over the ceremony state the renderer owns (#247, Q7), and it keeps `OpenScreen`'s button.
6. **A schema, a description, and a reason.** Every tool declares a flat JSON Schema: `op` (an enum) plus the named arguments of the operation; its description lists each operation, what it does, and which of them write. The arguments are validated per operation in the main process before the handler runs, so an out-of-shape call is a `ToolError` the model reads and recovers from, not a crash. **Every writing operation takes `why`**, one sentence of at most 300 characters: the model's stated reason, kept in the audit as its claim (rule 22).
7. **Offered to both engines the way the other app tools are.** They are `ToolImpl`s in the call's `runnerTools`: the open engine runs them directly, the Claude SDK gets them as the in-process server it gets `CallAgent` as. They are `principalOnly`: a sub-agent of the persona never has them, and **on the Claude path a sub-agent guard** like the one the memory's write tools have (`src/main/agents.ts:860`) is needed, because the SDK path has no `principalOnly`. They are offered to **the persona's real entry only**: never to its test copy (rule 11 of #247), never to another agent whatever its permission, and not to an agent the persona calls. Because the Claude path runs a tool with no abort signal, **the door, not the tool context, carries the stop** (rule 30).
8. **What a tool returns.** Text for the model: masked with the app's redaction, cut at a stated size with the note the other tools use, and, for text that comes from outside the app (an issue's body, a comment, a page, a plugin's output), inside the data fence the prompts use. A refusal (a handler's, the test workspace's, the door's) is worded by the app and returned as a tool error. A tool never returns a secret's value except the one operation of rule 9.
9. **Secrets.** The persona lists them (names and sources, as the screens do), checks, sets and removes them through the same channels, and may **read a value** through the door-only operation. The value reaches the model, hence the model's provider, which is the maintainer's decision; what the app adds is that **a value the persona read is masked by value in everything stored or shown from that answer** (the answer, the text being written, the activity and action lines, the record of what was read, the audit) and that every read is audited as the persona's with the name and nothing else. #251 gives agents use of a secret by reference; this does not.
10. **Settings, the team, autonomy and its own permissions.** The `Settings` tool changes the configuration **one path at a time**: the app reads the current configuration, applies the change, validates the whole (the same check a save runs) and saves, so a long document is never sent through the model. Any field may be changed, including `agents.team[<persona>]`'s `permission`, `tracker`, `shell`, `screen`, hosts and browser profile, and the autonomy blocks. What stays is the **shape of its entry**, which is validation for everyone (#247 rule 3): one persona, with no stage, squad, `turnsTo`, `autonomous` or `system`. That is the persona's place outside the cycle, not a limit on what it may do. A save that raises reach (the checks of `raisedPermissions`, `screenRaised`, `poolRaised`, `raisedAutonomy`, used here to mark and not to refuse) is **marked `raised`** in the audit line and in the act line of the chat, with the field names.
11. **The persona's own writes in the conversation are not proposals.** Its answer stays plain text with no `proposals` (#247 rule 32); a write on the code host is made by a tool, and is whatever the handler makes of it: a proposal that waits in Actions (the board's autonomy off, the default) or a direct audited write (on). A proposal made under the persona origin records that it was (rule 16), and the persona may approve it with `Actions`.

### The persona origin

12. **A third origin.** `callOrigin()` returns `'ipc'`, `'web'` or `'persona'`. The door runs the handler through the same wrapper every call goes through (`guarded`: the error log, the job id, the activity context), with `persona` as its origin. The HTTP door still labels its calls `'web'`. A single helper replaces the six comparisons of the table above and returns `window`, `paired` or `persona`; `AnsweredThrough` and `TurnAnswerer` gain `persona` (the turn-limit comment already leaves room for it), and so do the memory audit's `via` and the persona editor's `through`. A test fails when a handler compares the origin with `'web'` directly.
13. **The origin lasts as long as the act.** The act's scope closes when the handler's promise settles. Work the handler leaves running (a run it started, a timer, an announced plugin write) is **not** the persona's: the run's agents act under their own names, and the lines they write say what they say today. The run, the proposal or the thread that the persona started carries the fact that the persona started it (rules 15 and 16), which is how the later lines are traced back.
14. **The door the request came by.** An answer remembers whether the message it answers came from the window or from a paired browser, and every audit line of the persona says so (`door`). The persona asked from the phone has the reach of rule 2, and the audit shows that it was asked from there. The field is open to more values, and #250's schedule is one.
15. **Records worded as the person's.** Wherever the app writes "the person did this" because of a person's call, a call under the persona origin writes "the persona" instead: the thread messages and history of a run's answer, acceptance, return, gate and send-back (`author`, and `by` in the history, a free string); a memory note or a procedure marked as reviewed by the person; the output of an Actions card. A post made through `Conversations` is **authored by the persona, never as the person**, and calls nobody (an agent's text never calls another agent, `callsOf`). The plan lists the sites from a search, not from memory (the two person posts of the forum, the ceremony's author mapping and the run transitions today), and a test covers each.
16. **Proposals know who made them.** An action created under the persona origin has an optional `proposedBy: 'persona'` (absent: as today). Actions shows it, and the audit line of approving says it was approved by the same persona that proposed it.

### The audit

17. **Who is a field.** `AuditEntry` gains optional fields, all absent on a line written before: `actor` (`'persona'`; absent means a person, as every old line reads), `door` (`window` or `paired`), `id` (so the chat can link to the line), `said` (the persona's `why`) and `basis` (rule 22). `by` keeps its meaning (the agent whose autonomy let a write go out) and is never used for the approver, which a ceremony's command already shows would be ambiguous.
18. **Stamped in one place, written by the handler.** `recordWrite` stamps every line written **while a persona act is open** with `actor`, `door`, `id` and the act's `basis`, so the host writes of an approval, a hold answer's `screen-hold` line (its `through` becomes `persona`), the memory lines and the turn-limit line all say it without each handler learning to. The door writes a line of its own (`act`) only when the handler wrote none, so an act has one line and not two, except that an approval's several host writes are several lines, as today.
19. **The lines that did not exist.** Four kinds are new, and are written **for every origin, the person's included**, because an audit that says "the persona" must be able to say "the person" for the rest: `config`, `secret`, `plugin-decision` and `act`.
    - `config`: written by `config:save`, `config:cycle-save`, `config:prompt-set` and `config:import-apply`, and, for the persona, by the door around **every write act**, which compares the configuration before and after whatever channel changed it (so `runs:setAutonomous` is covered with no line of its own). It holds the changed paths (the first twenty and a count), the old and new value of booleans, numbers and short enumerations, the **length** of any other value (never a text: the persona's instructions, prompts and commands are not copied), and `raised` (rule 10).
    - `secret`: set, remove, accept-insecure and read, with the name and the source type; never a value, a command's arguments or a path.
    - `plugin-decision`: an answer (once, session, always, refuse), a revocation, a switch, a setting; the plugin and the need, never a secret or a request's text.
    - `act`: any persona act whose handler writes nothing (a run's accept, a gate, a priority on the board file, a post, a memory note...).
20. **What is not an act.** What the door refuses before it runs a handler (stopped, not in the table, arguments invalid) writes no audit line: nothing was attempted. It shows in the Work section as a tool error. A handler that refuses (the test workspace, a validation, an already-handled ask) is recorded as it records failures today, with `ok: false`.
21. **The Audit screen** gains a **Who** filter (all, person, persona), a badge on the persona's lines, the door, the `said` sentence, and `basis` folded, with the four new kinds labelled in both catalogs. The screen stays a reader: **no channel writes, edits or deletes the audit, and none is added**, so the persona has no way to rewrite what it did.

### What it read when it decided

22. **The record.** Each audit line of a persona **write** carries a `basis` of two parts, bounded and masked:
    - **`subject`**: what the app itself puts in front of a person to decide, read by the door at the moment of the act and not taken from the model: an action's summary and command, a hold's step in words and the site, a plugin request's need and plugin, the configuration paths about to change. It cannot be wrong because the model misread it.
    - **`reads`**: up to eight of the persona's **own read results in this answer**, newest first, those that name the same thing as the subject before the rest, each as tool, operation, target and an excerpt of at most 240 characters; plus the sequence number of the person's message the answer began from.
    - The whole is at most 2,000 characters. It is masked with the app's redaction and by value (rule 9) before it is written. A secret's value, a typed hand-off value and the instructions of the persona are never copied into it.
    - It is the persona's **tool results**, which is what reached the model, not its reasoning: the reasoning is a switch of the activity, memory-only, and is not an audit.
23. **Where it lives and how long.** In the audit line, so it lives as long as the line. The audit file is not swept by the retention today and the plan does not add a sweep; if one comes, a line keeps its `basis` for as long as the line (Q5).

### The chat shows what it did

24. **An act line.** Each persona write, successful or refused by the handler, adds a durable line to the persona's thread, worded by the app in the viewer's language (`tool`, `op`, a short masked subject of at most 120 characters, the result and the audit `id`), before the answer is posted. It is stored with the thread, so it shows on every device and after a restart, which the Work section does not (the activity lives in memory). A line for a persona raise says so. An answer has at most thirty such lines, then one line with the count of the rest, with the audit as the full record.
25. **Reads are not lines.** A read shows in the Work section as a step, as every tool does today (masked, with the same switch for reasoning), and in the `reads` of the next decision it informs.
26. **Links.** An act line opens the audit at its entry (the Audit screen scrolled to the `id`); a line about a run, a card, an action or a conversation also offers the button `OpenScreen` already makes for that target. No new navigation: a tap moves the device it was tapped on.
27. **What a person sees on the way.** The status strip's counts are unchanged and count what the persona has not resolved; the Actions list shows `proposedBy`; a conversation, a run's thread and the memory show the persona's name where they would have shown the person's.

### The stop

28. **State.** A **latch** per workspace: stopped or not, since when, and by which door. It is kept in a small file in the workspace's folder, **outside the configuration**, so the settings tool cannot reach it, an older app opens the workspace, and the schema does not change (Q6). It is read at start and an unreadable file counts as **stopped** (it fails closed). It is published to the window and every paired browser as a module event, and read with `persona:state`.
29. **Who can use it.** Two channels, `persona:stop` and `persona:resume`, and a read, `persona:state`, are open to a paired browser and are not external effects: stopping takes capability away, and resuming is the person's own act on their own persona. They are **not in the door's table**: the persona cannot stop, resume or change its own stop, directly or by writing the state (Q3). The per-answer Stop (`runs:callStop`, #247 rule 31) stays as it is and does not latch.
30. **What "at once" means.** Pressing Stop, before the call returns:
    1. sets the latch, so no new tool call starts and no new answer begins;
    2. aborts **every call registered in the persona's thread** (the persona's answer, and every agent it called, which register in the same thread), which also ends the model's request and the sub-agents of the call, and closes the shell session of the answer;
    3. refuses at the door any tool call that is already on its way: the door checks the latch in the same turn of the event loop in which it starts the handler, with no wait between the two, so a call cannot slip past it;
    4. posts a line in the thread: stopped, by the window or the phone, with the acts that were **in flight** (the door tracks them) and the words "they finish on their own".
    What is already happening in the app is **not undone**: a push or a write to the code host that `Actions` had begun finishes and is audited, exactly as if the person had clicked and then pressed Stop. The line says which were running, and the rest is read in the audit.
31. **A queued answer and a waiting ask.** Messages written while stopped are stored and shown as **waiting for Resume**; their answers do not start. A message that was already queued behind the answer that was stopped waits the same way. An ask that waits for the person (a browser hold, a plugin request, a turn-limit question, a ceremony's command) **stays pending for the person**, with its own clock (a hold counts as declined after fifteen minutes): stopping the persona answers nothing on the person's behalf. A persona's answer that is itself waiting for a person's turn-limit decision is ended by the abort.
32. **Resume.** The same control. It clears the latch, posts a line, and **starts the waiting answers in order**. The waiting is in memory like every queue today: if the app restarted while stopped, the state is still stopped (it is on disk), and the messages that were waiting are shown as **not answered** after Resume, for the person to write again; nothing runs from a request the person made before they stopped it unless they let the app's queue carry it.
33. **Where it is.** The chat's header on the computer and on the phone (always visible, and a **banner** over the box while stopped: "The persona is stopped. Messages wait until you resume it"); a small indicator in the app's shell on every other screen **while it is working or stopped**, with the same control; and Settings › Persona, which shows the state and offers Resume. It uses theme tokens and both catalogs. The wizard's test copy is not stopped by it: it cannot act.
34. **A stopped persona is not a disabled persona.** The person can still read the conversation, edit the persona, clear it or delete it (those are refused only while an answer is going). Reads of the app by the person, the audit included, work as always.

### The test workspace

35. **The guard is inside the handlers and stays there.** The door adds no guard and removes none. In a workspace with `test: true`, every persona act that leaves the machine is refused by the same `assertExternalWrite` with the same message, returned as a tool error, and recorded by the handler with `ok: false`. Everything that is local to the machine (the board's file, the configuration, memory, secrets, the audit) is as open to the persona there as it is to the person, and the hosts, persistent profile and the app's browser of agents that run on the computer are withdrawn as the test workspace rule says, including from the persona if it gives itself them. The workspace's test flag and the test-environment confirmations are not in the door (rule 3, Q2). The tools are offered in a test workspace; a test persona's chat can be used to try them.

### While the person is away

36. **What "away" is in this issue.** The persona acts only inside an answer to a message, so it acts while the person is away in three ways: a long answer that goes on after they put the phone down, the answers queued behind it, and the agents it called. There is **no human in the loop by decision**: nothing asks the person before a persona act, and an act the person would have to approve (a push, a hold) is approved by the persona itself. #250's schedules will be a fourth way, through the same door, the same origin, the same stop and the same audit.
37. **What the person sees afterwards.** In the persona's conversation: the answer, and above it, in order, the line of every act it made (rule 24), each linking to the audit with the reason and what it had read. The conversation's unread mark and the existing notifications are how the person learns something happened; this issue adds no new notification. The Audit screen filtered to the persona is the complete record, and the Actions list shows what it proposed, approved or set aside. Stopping from the phone while away is the control.
38. **Nothing runs from a stopped state.** An answer that was in progress when the app closed is gone, as it is today; a stopped persona stays stopped across a restart (rule 28).

### The phone and the configuration

39. **The phone gets the control and the chat, and nothing else new.** `persona:stop`, `persona:resume` and `persona:state` join `PERSONA_CHANNELS` (open to a paired browser, not external effects), pinned by the policy test. The tools are not channels and give a browser nothing it did not have; but a message to the persona from a paired browser makes the persona act with the reach of rule 2, and the audit says `door: paired`.
40. **No schema step.** This issue does not change the schema: whatever version the release it lands on carries, there is no step of its own. The latch is a file outside the configuration (rule 28) and the tools need no setting, so nothing here needs a step. The persona's review line in the wizard and its Settings text, which say it can read, chat and open screens, are corrected to say what it can do now, and the persona's documentation says it acts, in both languages (Q6).

## Out of scope

- **Voice in the chat** (#249), **schedules** (#250), **plugins, MCP servers and secrets by reference** (#251), and **a budget, a spend cap or a rate limit** (#198).
- **Holding the pre-daily** or any ceremony turn written by the persona: the ceremony state is the renderer's (#247, Q7). The persona opens it with a button and drives the ceremonies' main-side channels.
- **Proposals in the persona's answer** (#247 rule 32): its writes are tool calls.
- **A write channel for the audit**, an export of it, a retention sweep of it, or a notification for each persona act.
- **A second persona, or a persona per device**; **undoing** an act; **an approval step before a persona act** (settled against).
- **Attribution inside text sent to the code host** (Q7) and **changing a run's format**: the history's `by` is already free text.
- **The persona's use of its own memory tools and the learned procedures' agent tools**, which exist and are unchanged; `Memory` adds the person's side (review, restore, delete).

## Acceptance criteria

Each one is a check a person makes on screen or by test, with the data folder pointed at an empty one and a fake model.

1. **The four from the issue.** From the chat, with a fake model that calls the tools: the persona changes a setting, moves a card's priority, approves a proposal and answers a browser hold. The audit has four lines (more for the approval's host writes), each with `actor: 'persona'`, the door, its `said`, and a `basis`.
2. **Stop.** While a persona answer is running with a tool call pending, Stop returns with the latch set; the model call is aborted; the pending tool call is refused without running its handler; an agent the persona called is aborted too; the thread has the stopped line; a message written afterwards shows as waiting; Resume answers it.
3. **Stop is the person's.** `persona:stop`, `persona:resume` and `persona:state` are in the phone's allowed set and out of the external effects; no tool operation names them; a persona that asks to resume (a fake model) is told it cannot. The state survives a restart; an unreadable state file starts the persona stopped.
4. **Not undone.** With an approval in flight when Stop is pressed, the approval finishes and is audited, and the stopped line names it as in flight.
5. **The origin.** `callOrigin()` is `persona` inside a door call and `web` in the HTTP door; no source compares it with `'web'` outside the one helper; a hold's answer, a turn-limit answer and a memory edit made by the door say `persona` where the person's say `window` or `paired`.
6. **The act's scope.** A run started by the persona writes its own later audit lines without `actor`; the run's history says the persona started it.
7. **The table.** Every registered channel is in exactly one place, a tool operation or the exclusion list with a reason; a fake new channel fails the test. The door-only list is exactly the ones named in rule 4.
8. **The tools.** Each of the eleven has a schema, a description naming its operations, and a `why` on every writing operation; an invalid argument is a tool error; both engines are given the same names; a sub-agent, a called agent and the persona's test copy are given none; on the Claude path a sub-agent's call to one is refused.
9. **Approvals, with proposals.** A proposal made under the persona origin has `proposedBy`; approving it writes host lines stamped with the persona and the line says it was its own; approving by the persona when the workspace is a test one is refused with the usual message and `ok: false`.
10. **The audit says who for the lines that did not exist.** A person's `config:save` and `config:cycle-save` write a `config` line with paths and no text; the persona's change of a setting writes the same, plus `actor`, `door`, and, for a change of its own `shell`, `raised`; a secret set, removed and read writes a `secret` line with a name and no value; a plugin answer and a revocation write a `plugin-decision` line.
11. **What it read.** A decision's `basis` has the `subject` the door read itself (a test where the model misdescribes the proposal and the line still holds the real summary), at most eight reads, each at most 240 characters, the whole at most 2,000, the message number; a secret value the persona read, a typed hand-off value and a token are masked in it.
12. **Secrets.** Reading a secret's value returns it to the model and nothing else shows it: the stored answer, the text being written, the activity and act lines, the audit and the error log hold the masked form.
13. **The chat.** Each persona write puts a line in the thread, stored, in the viewer's language, with the audit link; after a restart the lines are there and the Work sections are not; more than thirty acts end in one count line; a refused act shows as refused.
14. **Not the person's.** A post made by `Conversations` is authored by the persona and calls no agent; a run's answer, accept, return, gate and send-back made by the persona say the persona in the thread and the history; the memory and procedure marks say so.
15. **Own permissions.** The persona raises its own `shell` through `Settings`; the save is accepted, the line says `raised` with the field, and a save that gives the persona a stage, a squad or `autonomous: true` is refused by validation with the usual message.
16. **Test workspace.** In a workspace with `test: true`, an approval, a board write and a push attempted by the persona are refused by the guard and recorded `ok: false`; a configuration change and a memory note succeed.
17. **Phone.** The same chat on a paired browser makes the persona act with the reach of the table (an act the phone cannot do alone, such as `plugins:answer`, succeeds through the persona) and the audit line says `door: paired`; the stop works from the phone with the external-effects switch off.
18. **The audit screen.** The Who filter, the badge, the door, the `said` and the folded `basis` show; the four new kinds are labelled in both catalogs; there is no channel that writes the audit.
19. **No schema change.** This issue adds no step: the schema version is the release's own (31 when it was brought up to date) and the issue's diff does not touch `schema.ts`, `types.ts`' version or the JSON Schema; a workspace written by this version opens in the previous one.
20. **Wording and theme.** Every new string is in both catalogs through `t()`, the tool descriptions are English prompt text marked as such, the control and the banner use theme tokens only, and the wizard's line and the persona documentation no longer say the persona cannot act.

## Open questions

Answered at gate 1 (2026-10-11): every question is settled with its recommendation, including Q2: the excluded channels (the test workspace switch and the test-secret confirmations among them) stay out of the persona's reach.

Each has a recommendation; the maintainer decides at this gate.

**Q1. Tool granularity: one tool per channel, or task-level tools.**
*Recommendation: task-level tools, by area, with an `op` (rule 5).* About 280 tools would fill the model's context, make the choice among them unreliable, and give the activity and audit lines nothing readable. Eleven tools, each with a flat schema and the operations in the description, keep the model's list short. The table in the door still names every channel, and its test is what stops an area from drifting. The alternative, one tool per writing channel (some forty), reads better in an activity line and gives each a tighter schema; it costs context on every answer. A middle path, task-level reads and one tool per *decision* (`ApproveAction`, `AnswerAsk`, `ChangeSetting`), is possible with no change to the rest of this spec.

**Q2. Which channels are excluded.**
*Recommendation: the list in rule 3, and in particular to keep two that depart from "no limits" out of the door:* the workspace's **test flag** (`workspace:test`) and the **test-environment confirmations** (`config:testenv-confirm`, `-revoke`). They are the guard that exists so an agent can be tried without writing anywhere; a persona that can lower it makes every test workspace a normal one, and the code already says the confirmation is "never offered to the agent". The maintainer's decision says the persona can do everything a person can; this is the one place the recommendation is to read it as "everything a person does as an owner of the machine, not as the person's representative". If the answer is to include them, the audit and the Stop are unchanged; only the table changes. The same reading excludes pairing a device, installing an update and the setup's dialogs.

**Q3. May the persona change the stop?**
*Recommendation: no, never* (rules 28 and 29). The stop is the person's control of the persona; one that the controlled party can release is not a control. The latch is outside the configuration for the same reason. This does not stop a persona that gave itself `shell: host` from changing a file of the machine (Risks): the stop and the audit are limits of the app's doors, not a cage around a host shell the person let the persona have.

**Q4. Rate or cost bounds.**
*Recommendation: none in this issue.* Each persona answer is bounded by the turn limit every conversation agent already has (a question to the person at the limit, which the persona's own paused call cannot answer for itself); the budget, autonomy and accounts of #198 are the place for a cap in money or acts per hour. The Stop and the 30-line cap of rule 24 are the only bounds added here.

**Q5. What "what it read" stores, and for how long.**
*Recommendation: rule 22 as written:* the subject the app read itself, up to eight of the persona's own tool results as short masked excerpts, the message it answers, 2,000 characters, kept with the audit line, which is not swept. The alternative of keeping the model's reasoning is rejected (it is memory-only and a switch). Keeping each read in full would put the persona's reads (issue bodies, memory) into a file that is not swept. If a retention for the audit is added later, the `basis` should be allowed to go first and the line stay.

**Q6. The schema step.**
*Recommendation: none; this issue adds no schema step* (rule 40). If the maintainer wants the tools **off for a persona saved before this release and on for a new one**, as a gate for those who tried the beta that "only chats", the cost is a boolean on the persona's entry and a step `30 → 31` that raises nothing and writes `false` for an existing persona; the persona's docs and the wizard's line would say so. I do not recommend it: the persona has not shipped in a stable release, and the maintainer's decision is that it has no fixed limits.

**Q7. Text the persona writes to the code host.**
Its comments, issue bodies and descriptions go out under the person's token, so the host shows the person as the author, which is "reading as the person's" outside the app. *Recommendation: a fixed one-line footer on text the persona composes for the host* (the audit already says who for the app's own records), worded by the app and not by the model. Whether the runner's own comments carry a signature today was not checked; the plan checks it and reuses the form. The alternative is to accept the host's author line as the person's and rely on the audit.

## Risks

- **Self-approval and self-elevation are the design.** A persona that approves its own proposals and raises its own reach is what was asked for; the controls are the audit, `proposedBy`, `raised`, and the Stop. None of them prevents an act; all of them make it findable.
- **A host shell the persona gave itself ends the guarantees.** With `shell: host`, the persona can edit the audit file, the latch and the secrets file with commands that are no app door. The app still asks the person for host commands that no rule allows, but `allowedCommands` is configuration the persona can change. The audit line of that change is `raised`; beyond that this is accepted, and it is the reason the stop and the audit are described as limits of the app's doors.
- **Injected text leads to an act.** An issue, a comment, a page or a plugin's output the persona reads can tell it to approve or to reveal. Fencing the text (rule 8) lowers the chance; the audit's `reads` shows what it read, and the Stop is the answer. The persona's instructions that say what it never wants done are the person's first defence.
- **The phone has the full reach.** A message from a paired browser makes the persona do what the phone alone cannot (`plugins:answer`, `config:save`), and the external-effects switch no longer decides it. This is settled; the audit says `door: paired`, and a lost phone is dealt with by unpairing it, which the persona cannot do.
- **Origin and async work.** The act's scope (rule 13) depends on closing it when the handler settles; an `AsyncLocalStorage` store follows work started inside it. A test pins that a run the persona starts writes its lines unstamped.
- **Records worded as the person's.** A site missed leaves a run's thread or a memory note saying the person did what the persona did. The plan lists them from a search; a person who relies on the thread to know "I answered this" is the person at risk.
- **The Claude path has no abort signal in a tool and no sub-agent exclusion.** The door carries the stop, and a guard hook for the persona's tools is needed (rule 7); if the guard cannot be made, the persona's tools are offered on the open engine only and the Claude engine says so in the thread, as the memory does.
- **A write already sent cannot be taken back.** The Stop halts the next step. The line of in-flight acts is the honest record of the rest.
- **Reading a secret sends it to a provider.** Settled, and the reason the read is audited and masked everywhere stored.
- **The queue is in memory.** A restart while stopped loses the messages that were waiting to be answered (they stay in the thread, marked not answered).
- **An audit that grows.** Every change of configuration and every persona act adds a line, and the file is never swept; the limit of the screen (2,000 newest) hides older lines from view, not from the file. A sweep is a decision of its own.
- **Prompt and tool texts.** The tool descriptions and the persona's prompt paragraph that teaches them are English text sent to models, marked as such for the i18n lint; a golden test of the prompt, if any covers the persona's call, changes with them.

## Priority and milestone proposed

- **Priority: `priority:high`.** It is what makes the persona more than a chat, and the stop and the audit are what make that safe to ship; #249, #250 and #251 reuse the door, the origin and the stop. A proposal; the person accepts or refuses.
- **Milestone: the next beta of `0.10.0`**, the release the work is based on. No schema step. The release note says what the person sees: the persona acts in the app on their behalf, every act is in the chat and in the audit under its name with the reason and what it had read, the audit also records the person's own configuration, secret and plugin changes, and one Stop halts the persona on the computer and on the phone.
